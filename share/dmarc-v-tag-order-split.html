Rspamd discards a DMARC record whose v= tag is not first while OpenDMARC enforces it, so tag order decides whether a domain is protected (OpenDMARC <= 1.4.2 and Rspamd ≤ 4.1.2)

Author: Weitong Li, Virginia Tech <weitongli@vt.edu>
Date: 2026-07-31
Vendor: Rspamd project (Vsevolod Stakhov) (fail-open side) and The Trusted Domain Project (specification-violating side)
Software: Rspamd and OpenDMARC
Affected version: Rspamd 4.1.2, originally isolated on 3.10.2, is the fail-open side. OpenDMARC 1.4.2 over-enforces, which is a specification violation in the safe direction.
Vulnerable component: DMARC record validation — the requirement that v= be the first tag
Vulnerability type: CWE-436 (Interpretation Conflict)
Attack vector: Remote, unauthenticated, when the victim's record already has the tags out of order; otherwise the attacker must be able to prepend a tag to the published record.
CVSS v3.1: 5.9 (Medium) — CVSS:3.1/AV:N/AC:H/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:N/I:H/A:N
Status: Reproduced on 2026-07-28 against the current release (run 20260728-120957) with a scripted reproducer, negative controls, and a second independent implementation as the differential oracle. Three repetitions, single _dmarc TXT record at a .com organisation, with the tag-order control.

Impact. A record such as p=reject; v=DMARC1 is treated as no DMARC by Rspamd and delivers the forgery, while OpenDMARC still rejects it.

Description

The exposure is asymmetric, and the entry is written accordingly. OpenDMARC deviates from the specification in the stricter direction: a record with v= out of order still protects the domain, so its deviation is not attacker-useful.

The fail-open lands on Rspamd. A domain whose published record has the tags out of order — through a typo, a content-management template that reorders tags, or an injection that prepends a tag — is treated as having no DMARC and becomes spoofable on Rspamd receivers, while OpenDMARC receivers still block the same message. The domain owner sees protection working on some receivers and not others, with no signal as to why.

RFC 7489 section 6.6.3 step 2 says records that do not start with a v= tag are discarded, and section 6.3 with the section 6.4 ABNF places v first positionally.

Rspamd follows this and discards such a record, returning DMARC_NA. OpenDMARC parses past the ordering and enforces the policy it finds. The two therefore disagree about whether a domain has a DMARC policy at all, and the fail-open exposure lands on Rspamd.

A single _dmarc TXT record at a .com organisation, forged unaligned mail, three repetitions:

_dmarc recordOpenDMARCRspamd
v=DMARC1; p=reject (control)dmarc=fail, p=rejectDMARC_POLICY_REJECT
p=reject; v=DMARC1dmarc=fail, p=rejectDMARC_NA — no valid record, delivered
adkim=s; v=DMARC1; p=rejectdmarc=failDMARC_NA
sp=none; v=DMARC1; p=rejectdmarc=failDMARC_NA

The only difference from the consensus baseline is that v= is no longer first, which isolates tag order as the sole cause.

A secondary case: v=DMARC1; p=; p=reject and v=DMARC1;; p=reject make OpenDMARC resolve reject while Rspamd returns DMARC_POLICY_SOFTFAIL with no disposition.

Several related hypotheses were swept and found to be consensus, and are recorded here so the finding is not overstated: a duplicate p= is taken as the last by both; p=Reject in the wrong case is accepted by both, correctly, since ABNF literals are case-insensitive; and p=foobar makes both apply no policy.

Proof of Concept

_dmarc.victim.example.  IN  TXT  "p=reject; v=DMARC1"

Send a forged, unaligned message with From: ceo@victim.example. Rspamd returns DMARC_NA and delivers; OpenDMARC returns dmarc=fail and rejects.

Mitigation

Rspamd is behaving as the specification requires, so the practical remedy is operational: emit a diagnostic that distinguishes “a TXT record exists at _dmarc but was discarded as invalid” from “no record exists”, so the misconfiguration is visible to the domain owner rather than silently equivalent to having no policy. OpenDMARC should additionally enforce the first-tag requirement so that the two agree.

Notes

This is the opposite direction to the other DMARC record-parsing findings in this set, where OpenDMARC fails open and Rspamd is correct. Here OpenDMARC over-enforces and Rspamd is the fail-open side. It is included because the cross-verifier disagreement is the security-relevant fact: one receiver blocks the forgery and the other delivers it.

Because Rspamd's behaviour is specification-conformant, a vendor may reasonably decline this as a defect. It is submitted as an interoperability and diagnosability issue rather than as an implementation flaw.
