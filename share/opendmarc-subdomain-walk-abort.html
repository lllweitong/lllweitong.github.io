OpenDMARC abandons the walk to the organisational domain when a subdomain publishes a wrong-version DMARC record, so sp=reject is never applied (OpenDMARC ≤ 1.4.2)

Author: Weitong Li, Virginia Tech <weitongli@vt.edu>
Date: 2026-07-31
Vendor: The Trusted Domain Project
Software: OpenDMARC
Affected version: OpenDMARC 1.4.2, built from the upstream tarball rel-opendmarc-1-4-2. The finding was originally isolated on the Debian package 1.4.2-1+deb12u1 and re-confirmed against pristine upstream. Earlier 1.4.x and 1.3.x releases are expected to share the behaviour.
Vulnerable component: DMARC policy discovery — the fall-through from the subdomain node to the organisational domain
Vulnerability type: CWE-755 (Improper Handling of Exceptional Conditions)
Attack vector: Remote, unauthenticated, when the subdomain already carries a malformed record; otherwise the attacker must be able to publish at the subdomain's _dmarc node.
CVSS v3.1: 7.5 (High) — CVSS:3.1/AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:N/I:H/A:N
Status: Reproduced on 2026-07-28 against the current release (run 20260728-120957) with a scripted reproducer, negative controls, and a second independent implementation as the differential oracle. Confirmed under a real .net registrable suffix, so the laboratory's public-suffix fixture cannot confound the result.

Impact. A typo such as v=DMARC2 at a subdomain's _dmarc node makes OpenDMARC permerror instead of falling through to the parent's sp=reject.

Description

A subdomain whose only protection comes from the parent's sp=reject — the very common p=none; sp=reject and organisation-only deployments — becomes fully spoofable on OpenDMARC receivers whenever a typo'd or wrong-version DMARC TXT record sits at the subdomain's _dmarc node. v=DMARC2 and v=DMARC1.0 are realistic mistakes for an administrator to make.

Where an attacker can publish at the subdomain — a delegated sub-zone, a tenant-controlled CNAME — publishing one wrong-version record is a deliberate way to switch off the parent's subdomain policy.

RFC 7489 section 6.6.3 step 2 requires that records not starting with a v= tag identifying the current version of DMARC are discarded, and step 3 requires that if the set is then empty the receiver MUST query the organisational domain. A v=DMARC2 or v=DMARC1.0 record at a subdomain must therefore be discarded and the walk continued, so that the organisation's sp= applies.

OpenDMARC returns permerror and aborts. The organisation's sp=reject is never consulted and the forged subdomain mail is delivered.

Forged, unaligned mail with From: ceo@sub.victim.net; the organisation publishes _dmarc.victim.net = v=DMARC1; p=reject; sp=reject; only the subdomain node varies:

_dmarc.sub.victim.netOpenDMARCRspamdRequired
(absent)dmarc=fail (walks to the organisation, applies sp=reject)DMARC_POLICY_REJECTfail
hello world (not a v=DMARC record)dmarc=fail (discards, walks to the organisation)DMARC_POLICY_REJECTfail
v=DMARC2; p=nonedmarc=permerror — the organisation's sp= is never consultedDMARC_POLICY_REJECTfail
v=DMARC1.0; p=nonedmarc=permerrorDMARC_POLICY_REJECTfail

The two controls are what make this precise. An absent record, and a record that is not a v=DMARC record at all, both make OpenDMARC walk to the organisation correctly and return fail. OpenDMARC therefore does implement discard-then-walk; it specifically misroutes a v=DMARC<not-1> token to permerror and aborts the walk.

Proof of Concept

_dmarc.victim.net.       IN  TXT  "v=DMARC1; p=reject; sp=reject"
_dmarc.sub.victim.net.   IN  TXT  "v=DMARC2; p=none"

Send a forged, unsigned message with From: ceo@sub.victim.net from an unrelated host with an envelope sender in another domain. Required: the subdomain record is discarded, the walk reaches victim.net, sp=reject applies, and the verdict is dmarc=fail. Observed on OpenDMARC: dmarc=permerror, delivered.

Mitigation

Discard a record whose v= does not match the current version and continue policy discovery to the organisational domain, per section 6.6.3 steps 2 and 3. The existing handling of an absent record and of a non-DMARC record already does the right thing; the wrong-version case should join them rather than routing to permerror.

Notes

Shares its root cause with opendmarc-bad-tag-drops-policy, which covers single-node tag validity with the From at the organisation. The distinct contribution here is the failure of the step-3 fall-through and its geometry: the bad record sits at the subdomain and its permerror suppresses the organisation's sp=. Both should be fixed by the same change to the permerror-versus-discard logic.
