OpenDMARC applies one record from a multi-record _dmarc set instead of discarding the set, enabling policy downgrade (OpenDMARC ≤ 1.4.2)

Author: Weitong Li, Virginia Tech <weitongli@vt.edu>
Date: 2026-07-31
Vendor: The Trusted Domain Project
Software: OpenDMARC
Source code: https://github.com/trusteddomainproject/OpenDMARC
Affected version: OpenDMARC 1.4.2, built from the upstream tarball rel-opendmarc-1-4-2. The finding was originally isolated on the Debian package 1.4.2-1+deb12u1 and re-confirmed against pristine upstream. Earlier 1.4.x and 1.3.x releases are expected to share the behaviour.
Vulnerable component: DMARC policy discovery — multi-record handling, in libopendmarc's opendmarc_policy_query_dmarc()
Vulnerability type: CWE-290 (Authentication Bypass by Spoofing)
Attack vector: Remote. The attacker must be able to place a competing TXT record at the victim's _dmarc node, or to influence the order of the DNS response.
CVSS v3.1: 5.9 (Medium) — CVSS:3.1/AV:N/AC:H/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:N/I:H/A:N
Status: Reproduced on 2026-07-28 against the current release (run 20260728-120957) with a scripted reproducer, negative controls, and a second independent implementation as the differential oracle. 14 of 14 cases in the original run; the wire-order behaviour was separately characterised against a stock BIND 9 authoritative server.

Impact. When two DMARC records exist at the same node, OpenDMARC applies whichever arrives first on the wire rather than treating the domain as having no policy.

Description

A DMARC policy downgrade. An actor who can publish a competing TXT record at the victim's _dmarc node flips receiver enforcement from p=reject or p=quarantine to p=none. Realistic routes to that position include a sub-delegated _dmarc zone served by a tenant, cache poisoning against a recursive resolver downstream of the receiver, and an on-path attacker between OpenDMARC and its configured nameserver.

Success is probabilistic at roughly one in two against a random-ordering authoritative server, and higher where the attacker controls zone order or a cache. For a high-volume campaign, half is more than sufficient. The honest framing is a probabilistic policy downgrade, not a deterministic bypass.

RFC 7489 section 6.6.3 states that if the remaining record set contains multiple records, “processing terminates and the Mail Receiver takes no action”; RFC 9989 section 4.7 restates it as a MUST NOT. The set is to be discarded and the domain treated as having no policy.

OpenDMARC filters the TXT records for those beginning v=DMARC1 and applies the first one its parser accepts, without checking whether the set is ambiguous. It then reports dmarc=pass or dmarc=fail computed from that single record's tags — a verdict the specification forbids from being produced at all.

The rule is “first DMARC1 record parsed from the DNS response”. Rspamd correctly emits DMARC_BAD_POLICY for any multi-record set.

Wire order in production is probabilistic. The behaviour was re-measured against a stock BIND 9 authoritative server carrying both records in its zone file, queried twelve times in succession:

Record returned firstCount
p=reject first7 of 12
p=none first5 of 12

BIND 9's default rrset-order is random, as are NSD's and Knot DNS's. The specification violation is unconditional, but the downgrade lands on roughly half of queries against a typical authoritative server unless the attacker also controls zone order or an intermediate cache.

Proof of Concept

_dmarc.victim.example.  IN  TXT  "v=DMARC1; p=none"
_dmarc.victim.example.  IN  TXT  "v=DMARC1; p=reject"

Send a message with From: alice@victim.example. Required: the set is discarded, no policy is applied, and the verdict is dmarc=none. Observed on OpenDMARC:

Authentication-Results: receiver.test; dmarc=pass (p=none dis=none)
    header.from=victim.example

The verifier picked the p=none record and applied it.

Mitigation

After filtering for records beginning v=DMARC1, if more than one remains, discard the set and treat the domain as having no policy, per RFC 7489 section 6.6.3 and RFC 9989 section 4.7. Emitting a diagnostic, as Rspamd's DMARC_BAD_POLICY does, helps operators find the misconfiguration.

Notes

The specification violation is unconditional and reproduces deterministically; the exploitation is probabilistic because the attacker cannot generally force wire order. Both facts are stated here deliberately so that the entry is not read as a deterministic bypass.

OpenDMARC's manual page documents no option to enforce multi-record rejection, and the shipped default configuration does not do so.
